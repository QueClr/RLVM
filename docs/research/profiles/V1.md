# V1: a worker in steady state allocates nothing

Agent "V1" (memory behaviour of the machine engine), 2026-11-24, 18:37Z onwards. Question (P7-F1): the collections
are the 5 - 8 % between one process with 8 workers and the hardware ceiling; who makes the garbage, and can a
worker in steady state allocate (almost) nothing?

Short answer. The machine's part of the garbage is not "arrays regrown after each reset" but **the 1 - 2 % of the
sessions whose one declaration needs more than the kept slab sizes** (8 MiB of records, 4 MiB of run words): each of
them doubles its slabs copy by copy and drops them at its reset. On mathlib that is 4.9 GB of slabs and 0.56 GB of
tables per pass = 39 % of the 13.8 GB the 8 workers allocate. The prototype gives these arrays a **process-wide pool**
(taken at the growth, given back at the session's reset, softly held, expiring after 8,192 resets), hands the arrays
of a closed session to the next session of the process, and counts every allocation. Measured (section 5): the
machine then allocates PENDING. The other 61 % of the workers' bytes is the **reference kernel
re-checking the units the machine declines** (`rlvm.ref.IntIntMap` 21 %, the store overlay's index 17 %, the rewriter
memo 11 %, the overlay's node array 9 %): three small patches are proposed to their owners and were measured in
private copies. The region question (section 6): the session already is two bump regions that a reset rewinds; a
reset costs PENDING; what a two-region design could buy is reuse (ticks), not memory.

Everything here is a prototype in private copies (`work/v1/`); nothing was published.

## 2. The account: who allocates what

### 2.1 mathlib, one batch pass, 8 workers (JVM, 13 GB heap, announced; `base-mathlib-t8.alloc.txt`)

17.4 GB in all: workers 13.83 GB, loader thread 3.11 GB, environment thread 0.42 GB (exact). The workers' bytes:

| Site | GB | Share | What it is |
|---|--:|--:|---|
| `Machine.copyOrNull` < `growRecords` / `growRuns` | 4.89 | 35 % | slab doublings ABOVE the kept sizes: record slab to 16 MiB x 71, 32 MiB x 27, 64 MiB x 4 (2.37 GB); run slab to 8 MiB x 123, 16 MiB x 35, 32 MiB x 11 (1.99 GB); below the kept sizes only the first growth of each worker (0.19 GB) |
| `Machine.newTable` | 0.56 | 4 % | intern tables above the 4 MiB pool (8 MiB x 29, 16 MiB x 4), pair tables above it (8 MiB x 7, 16 MiB x 1), and each worker's first table of every size |
| `rlvm.ref.IntIntMap.allocate` | 2.89 | 21 % | the reference kernel's caches: cut back to 256 entries at `clear()` when large or sparse, doubled again in the next large declaration (100,000 tables of 4 KiB - 16 MiB) |
| `Overlay.rehash` (store) | 2.37 | 17 % | the hash-consing index of the overlay: cut back to 128 KiB by `resetTo` (4,354 new tables), doubled again by `insert` (3,567 new tables of 256 KiB - 64 MiB) |
| `TermOps.Memo.widen` (store) | 1.54 | 11 % | two temporaries per doubling of the rewriter's memo; every call starts at 64 slots |
| `Overlay.grow` + `releaseNodes` (store) | 1.40 | 10 % | the overlay's node array: 0.9 GB is ONE declaration of the reference kernel that grows it to 256 MiB; the rest the first growth of every lane, and four shrinks back to 32 MiB |
| level overlays (`IntListStore`, `LevelStore.<init>`) | 0.24 | 2 % | new lanes (3 per worker: machine, strict, reference) |
| everything else of the machine | < 0.01 | | per-unit set-up, statistics, literals, `Values.Ext`, `StrRed`: nothing |

Where the stack is complete (JFR keeps 64 frames; the reference kernel recurses deeper), the `IntIntMap`, `Overlay`
and `Memo` rows come from `Worker.reference > StoreLane.admit > RefSession.check`: the re-check of a unit the
machine declined (6,711 units of mathlib, the eager-reduction rule; agent E1 works on keeping them on the machine,
which would remove most of these four rows at the source).

**Correction of P7-F1**: the machine's session arrays are not grown again after each reset. Since F25 (NOTES-M6)
slabs are kept up to 8 MiB / 4 MiB and tables are pooled up to 4 MiB; a session at the 1 MiB cap needs 1 - 2 MiB of
records. What is grown again is the tail of the session-size distribution (machine census, mathlib slice / cedar /
Std: 2,300 - 2,500 of 3,200 - 5,200 sessions end with 16,000 - 32,000 records, 1 - 12 per corpus with more than
131,000): about 280 of the 14,000 sessions of a mathlib pass, 20 KB per unit on average but 17 MB per such session.

engines) for every pass: in the owner's warm metric it is paid in every pass.

## 3. The prototype (owner M6: `Machine.java`, `MachineSession.java`, `MachineEngine.java`)

Patch: `work/v1/patches/rlvm-machine-M6/0001-large-arrays-shared-spares-census.patch` (against the main tree as of
18:38Z; the three files were unchanged in the main tree when this was written). Parts, separable:

1. **Large arrays are shared** (`Machine.Big`, `Sizes.bigShared`, `-Drlvm.machine.big=shared|own|off`, default
   `shared`). A slab that grows past its kept size takes its next size from a process-wide pool (only the words in
   use are copied; a miss allocates as before) and the array goes back to the pool at the session's reset; the same
   for intern and pair tables above 2^20 slots (pooled all-zero). The session's own kept arrays and table pools are
   as before, so the reset path of the 98 % of the sessions is unchanged. Rules that make it safe:
   - an array that a growth replaced, or that the session is done with, is handed to the pool at the END of
     `reset()`, never while a judgment runs: code that still holds a replaced array (a local copy of `m.vh`) reads
     what it read before, exactly as when the old array was garbage (a first version gave it away at the growth);
   - not under a debug mode (`poison` wants zeroed slabs);
   - the pool holds its arrays through `SoftReference`s, and `Machine.heapAllows` empties it before it refuses a
     growth: pooled memory is never the reason for an `OutOfMemoryError` or for an `E_MEM` decline;
   - at most 2 arrays per size and pool (`-Drlvm.machine.big.perClass`), none above 128 MiB
     (`big.maxLog2`), and an array that no session took for 8,192 resets (all machines; `big.decay`) goes back to
     the heap: one huge declaration does not pin memory for ever, and what is held is a property of the process,
     not 8 workers times the largest declaration.
2. **Spare session arrays** (`Machine.release()`, `Machine.Spare`, `-Drlvm.machine.spares=N`, default 16, 0 = off):
   when a session is closed its machine is reset and its arrays (tables all zero, checked; slabs at most the kept
   size) go to a process-wide list, softly held; the next `MachineSession` starts with them instead of allocating
   and growing its own. A later pass of `-X passes`, a lane that is made again, the next file of a long-lived
   process pay the first growth once per process.
3. **Per-worker retention** (`Sizes.retainRecords / retainRunWords / retainTable / retainQuiet`,
   `-Drlvm.machine.big=own`): the first design, kept as a measured variant only: each machine keeps its slab at
   the size it grew to, up to 64 MiB, and gives it back after 1,024 quiet resets. Same garbage as (1), no copy at
   all in steady state, but the memory is held per worker (peak RSS +250 MiB on cedar and Std with 8 workers) and
   16 closed sessions holding such slabs ran a 6 GB JVM of the driver's invariance suite out of memory. Not the
   default; INT6 may drop it (`retained`, `decayPools`, `vhBig / rsBig`).
4. **Census** (always on, cold paths only): `machine.alloc.grow|reset|pooled.<array>.2^k`,
   `machine.alloc.newBytes`, `machine.alloc.growCopyBytes`, `machine.session.records|runWords.2^k`,
   `machine.resetNanos` / `machine.growNanos` (under statistics), `machine.big.*`, `machine.adopted`.
5. **A hazard found on the way, fixed in the patch** (F-V1-M2): `Machine.addPairAt` wrote the pair entry and then
   grew the pair log; a growth that the heap refuses (`HeapLimit`) left an entry in the table that the log does not
   know, `reset()` clears logged entries only, and a grown table goes to the pool as "all zero": a later session
   could read a pair answer of a dead session under the same record ids. Needs a refused growth of a log of 32 MiB
   or more (8 M pairs in one session); never seen. The patch logs first.

Not changed: record and run formats, ids, hashes, ticks; the base sizes; the session cap.
