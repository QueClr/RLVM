# V1: a worker in steady state allocates nothing

Agent "V1" (memory behaviour of the machine engine), 2026-11-24, 18:37Z onwards. Question (P7-F1): the collections
are the 5 - 8 % between one process with 8 workers and the hardware ceiling; who makes the garbage, and can a
worker in steady state allocate (almost) nothing?

Short answer. The machine's part of the garbage is not "arrays regrown after each reset" but **the 1 - 2 % of the
sessions whose one declaration needs more than the kept slab sizes** (8 MiB of records, 4 MiB of run words): each of
them doubles its slabs copy by copy and drops them at its reset. On mathlib that is 4.9 GB of slabs and 0.56 GB of
tables per pass = 39 % of the 13.8 GB the 8 workers allocate. The prototype gives these arrays a **process-wide pool**
(taken at the growth, given back at the session's reset, softly held, expiring after 8,192 resets), hands the arrays
of a closed session to the next session of the process, and counts every allocation. Measured (section 5): on cedar
the machine then allocates nothing from the second pass on (1,847 MB -> 382 MB over three passes, 374 MB of them in
the first), and a warm pipelined pass with 8 workers takes 2.39 s instead of 2.52 s on cedar, 1.81 instead of 1.88 s
on Std, **29.9 instead of 31.4 s on mathlib (-4.8 %)** for the same peak RSS. On mathlib the machine's garbage only
falls from 5.4 to 3.4 GB per pass at `-Xmx4g`, because a heap that is 2.8 GB live takes softly held arrays back at
every complete collection; the pause share stays near 10 %, so **the two targets of the brief (collections under
2 %, within 2 % of the ceiling) are not reached**. The other 61 % of the workers' bytes is the **reference kernel
re-checking the units the machine declines** (`rlvm.ref.IntIntMap` 21 %, the store overlay's index 17 %, the rewriter
memo 11 %, the overlay's node array 9 %): three small patches are proposed to their owners and were measured in
private copies; their first version ran the image out of the 4 GB heap, the patches are the bounded version. The
region question (section 6): the session already is two bump regions that a reset rewinds; a reset costs 8 - 9 us
(0.2 - 0.3 % of the worker CPU; the growths of the tables inside a session cost twenty times more); what a
two-region design could buy is reuse (ticks), not memory.

Everything here is a prototype in private copies (`work/v1/`); nothing was published.

Where the stack is complete (JFR keeps 64 frames; the reference kernel recurses deeper), the `IntIntMap`, `Overlay`
and `Memo` rows come from `Worker.reference > StoreLane.admit > RefSession.check`: the re-check of a unit the

which would remove most of these four rows at the source).

**Correction of P7-F1**: the machine's session arrays are not grown again after each reset. Since F25 (NOTES-M6)
slabs are kept up to 8 MiB / 4 MiB and tables are pooled up to 4 MiB; a session at the 1 MiB cap needs 1 - 2 MiB of
records. What is grown again is the tail of the session-size distribution (machine census, mathlib slice / cedar /
Std: 2,300 - 2,500 of 3,200 - 5,200 sessions end with 16,000 - 32,000 records, 1 - 12 per corpus with more than
131,000): about 280 of the 14,000 sessions of a mathlib pass, 20 KB per unit on average but 17 MB per such session.

### 2.3 What a reset and a growth cost in CPU

New statistics `machine.resetNanos` / `machine.growNanos` (wall time inside `Machine.reset()` and inside the
growths of slabs and tables; only under statistics).

| run (native image, 8 workers, pipelined, statistics on) | sessions (resets) | units per session | in use at a reset | inside resets | per reset | inside growths | worker CPU of the pass |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| cedar, quiet window, third pass (shared-2) | 4,987 (2,689 by the machine's cap) | 29 | 872 KB | 0.045 s | 9.1 us | 0.81 s | 17.3 s |
| Std, quiet window, third pass (shared-2) | 3,254 (2,124) | 31 | 965 KB | 0.029 s | 8.9 us | 0.56 s | 12.7 s |
| mathlib, quiet window, second pass (shared-2) | 59,534 (24,187) | 12 | 725 KB | 0.50 s | 8.4 us | 12.2 s | 216 s |
| mathlib, a host slot (loaded), one pass, base behaviour | 60,255 (23,691) | 12 | 716 KB | 1.14 s | 18.9 us | 31.9 s | |
| mathlib, a host slot (loaded), one pass, delivered tree | 60,190 (23,719) | 12 | 717 KB | 1.13 s | 18.8 us | 29.7 s | |

- **A reset is cheap**: 8 - 9 us on a quiet core, 0.2 - 0.3 % of the worker CPU (the tables are cleared in
  O(used); the slabs are not touched). P7's "14,000 resets per pass" was the 4 MiB cap; at the 1 MiB cap mathlib has
  60,000 sessions per pass, one per 12 units.
- **The growths cost twenty times more**: 4.4 - 5.6 % of the worker CPU is spent inside them (an upper bound: a
  collection that a growth's allocation starts is counted, 3.1 s of the 12.2 s on mathlib at most). Beyond the
  fresh arrays and the slab copies it is the re-probe of a session's records and runs when a table doubles,
  which happens in nearly every session: at the 1 MiB cap a session ends with 16,000 - 32,000 records and the
  base `vtab` has 2^15 slots (on cedar 2,666 of 5,058 sessions took the pooled 2^16 table, 794 the next `rtab`,
  730 the next pair table). Finding F-V1-M3. A base `vtab` of 2^16 slots alone
  (`-Drlvm.machine.sizes=14,16,16,14,12,10`, one row each in the same window) did not show: cedar 2.40 s against
  2.40 / 2.38 s, Std 1.78 s against 1.81 / 1.81 s (inside the spread). The tables to size are a sweep for M6
  (vtab, rtab and pairs together, against the cache), not done here.

## 3. The prototype (owner M6: `Machine.java`, `MachineSession.java`, `MachineEngine.java`)

Patch: `work/v1/patches/rlvm-machine-M6/0001-large-arrays-shared-spares-census.patch` (= `work/v1/tree5`, jar
4e129e8cb0c7; against the main tree as of 18:38Z; the three files were unchanged in the main tree at 20:40Z).
Parts, separable:

1. **Large arrays are shared** (`Machine.Big`, `Sizes.bigShared`, `-Drlvm.machine.big=shared|own|off`, default
   `shared`). A slab that grows past its kept size takes its next size from a process-wide pool (only the words in
   use are copied; a miss allocates as before) and the array goes back to the pool at the session's reset; the same
   for intern and pair tables above 2^20 slots (pooled all-zero). The session's own kept arrays and table pools are
   as before, so the reset path of the 98 % of the sessions is unchanged. Rules that make it safe:
   - an array that a growth replaced, or that the session is done with, is handed to the pool at the END of
     `reset()`, never while a judgment runs: code that still holds a replaced array (a local copy of `m.vh`) reads
     what it read before, exactly as when the old array was garbage (a first version gave it away at the growth).
     Until then it waits softly held, and an array above the pooled size is not kept at all, so a giant
     declaration needs no more memory than before (a version that held the replaced steps strongly - 16 + 32 +
     .. + 256 MiB under a 512 MiB slab - ran M8's gate out of its heap on `good/perf/magma-list-deep-n36`);
   - not under a debug mode (`poison` wants zeroed slabs);
   - the pool holds its arrays through `SoftReference`s, and `Machine.heapAllows` empties it before it refuses a
     growth: pooled memory is never the reason for an `OutOfMemoryError` or for an `E_MEM` decline;
   - the two pools hold at most a sixteenth of the maximum heap together, at most 512 MiB
     (`-Drlvm.machine.big.mib`), no array above 64 MiB (`big.maxLog2`), and an array that no session took for
     8,192 resets (all machines; `big.decay`) goes back to the heap: one huge declaration does not pin memory for
     ever, and what is held is a property of the process, not 8 workers times the largest declaration;
   - a slab may take a pooled array of up to four times the size it asked for (a larger slab costs nothing and
     saves the next doubling): the large sessions come in clusters, and the sizes on hand are rarely the ones
     asked for.
2. **Spare session arrays** (`Machine.release()`, `Machine.Spare`, `-Drlvm.machine.spares=N`, default 16, 0 = off):
   when a session is closed its machine is reset and its arrays (tables all zero, checked; slabs at most the kept
   size) go to a process-wide list, softly held; the next `MachineSession` starts with them instead of allocating
   and growing its own. A later pass of `-X passes`, a lane that is made again, the next file of a long-lived
   process pay the first growth once per process.
3. **Per-worker retention** (`Sizes.retainRecords / retainRunWords / retainTable / retainQuiet`,
   `-Drlvm.machine.big=own`): the first design, kept as a measured variant only: each machine keeps its slab at
   the size it grew to, up to 64 MiB, and gives it back after 1,024 quiet resets. Same garbage as (1), no copy at
   all in steady state, but the memory is held per worker (peak RSS +250 MiB on cedar and Std with 8 workers) and
   16 closed sessions holding such slabs ran a 6 GB JVM of the driver's invariance suite out of memory. Not the
   default; INT6 may drop it (`retained`, `decayPools`, `vhBig / rsBig`).
4. **Census** (always on, cold paths only): `machine.alloc.grow|reset|pooled.<array>.2^k`,
   `machine.alloc.newBytes`, `machine.alloc.growCopyBytes`, `machine.session.records|runWords.2^k`,
   `machine.resetNanos` / `machine.growNanos` (under statistics), `machine.big.*`, `machine.adopted`.
5. **A hazard found on the way, fixed in the patch** (F-V1-M2): `Machine.addPairAt` wrote the pair entry and then
   grew the pair log; a growth that the heap refuses (`HeapLimit`) left an entry in the table that the log does not
   know, `reset()` clears logged entries only, and a grown table goes to the pool as "all zero": a later session
   could read a pair answer of a dead session under the same record ids. Needs a refused growth of a log of 32 MiB
   or more (8 M pairs in one session); never seen. The patch logs first.

Not changed: record and run formats, ids, hashes, ticks; the base sizes; the session cap.

Two defects of EARLIER versions of the prototype, found by these checks and by the measurements, and what they
changed in the design:

- *Per-worker retention with strongly held spares* (the first prototype): two of three 6 GB JVMs of the
  invariance suite died with `OutOfMemoryError` (closed sessions' slabs of up to 64 MiB each, 16 sets), and the
  native image at `-Xmx4g` ended pass 2 of mathlib with "Garbage-collected heap size exceeded". Hence the shared,
  softly held pool with a bound per size and the rule that a spare never carries a slab above the kept size.
- *Giving a replaced slab to the shared pool at the growth*: code that holds a local copy of `m.vh` across a call
  that adds a record keeps reading the old array - harmless while the old array is garbage, wrong once another
  worker can write into it. Found by reading, before any run; hence the hand-over at the end of `reset()`.
- *Holding the replaced steps of a slab strongly until the reset* (the tree before the delivered one, jar
  1d6a18219223): M8's gate FAILED in its pipeline part with one `OutOfMemoryError`
  (`good/perf/magma-list-deep-n36`, a declaration whose slab passes 512 MiB: the replaced 16 .. 256 MiB arrays
  doubled what it held; `machine-gate-v3.md`). Everything else of that gate was clean. Hence the soft hold and
  the rule that an array above the pooled size (64 MiB) is not kept; the delivered tree passes.

live, and a complete collection under pressure takes softly held arrays - which is what "never the reason for an
OutOfMemoryError" means. So on mathlib at `-Xmx4g` the machine's garbage falls by 37 %, not by 90 %; in the 13 GB
JVM (account above, shared-2) by 61 %, on cedar by 98 % from the second pass on (JFR, three reload passes, 8
workers: 1,847 MB -> 382 MB, of which 374 MB in the first pass).

Allocation account of one batch pass with the prototype and the (first) proposals (JVM, JFR, exact per thread;
`x2-mathlib-t8.alloc.txt`): workers 13.83 -> **4.45 GB**, process 17.4 -> 7.6 GB, 18 -> 7 collections in the JVM.
By site: machine slabs 4.89 -> 1.76 GB and tables 0.56 -> 0.37 GB (shared-2; see below), `IntIntMap` 2.89 -> 0.21 GB,
overlay index 2.37 -> 0.42 GB, rewriter memo 1.54 -> 0.02 GB; untouched: `Overlay.grow` 1.19 GB, level overlays
0.24 GB, the loader 3.11 GB.

Reading:

- **The prototype is worth 4.8 % of the wall clock of a pipelined mathlib pass** (31.4 -> 29.9 s) and 3 % of the
  CPU, for the same peak RSS. Most of that is not the pauses (3.22 -> 2.89 s) but the work around a fresh 8 - 64
  MiB array: zeroing, page faults, the copy of the whole old slab instead of the words in use.
- **shared-2 misses too often**: the large sessions come in clusters (neighbouring units of the file are checked
  by all 8 workers at once), so more than two arrays of a size are wanted at the same time; of the run slabs to 8
  MiB 19 were still allocated (123 before), to 16 MiB 19 (35), record slabs to 16 MiB 19 (71), to 32 MiB 10 (27),
  to 64 MiB 4 (4): 2.1 GB of the machine's 5.4 GB remained. Hence the delivered version: the pool is bounded by its
  bytes (a sixteenth of the heap, at most 512 MiB) instead of 2 per size, and a slab takes a pooled array of up to
  four times the size it asked for.
- **Per-worker retention is not an option**: it holds 8 times the largest declaration, saved less (the large
  sessions are spread over the workers), and ran out of the 4 GB heap in the second pass.
- **The pools proposed for the store and the reference kernel must be small.** Their first version (index
  tables to 4 MiB per overlay, map tables to 8 MiB per map, 9 maps per kernel, 3 lanes per worker) ran the image
  out of the 4 GB heap in the first pass. The patches in `work/v1/patches/` are the bounded version (1 MiB per
  overlay index, 512 KiB per map table: 2 MiB and 12 MiB per lane at most); with it and the machine patch a
  pipelined mathlib pass frees 12.1 GB instead of 20.7 GB in 249 collections instead of 365, at the base's peak
  RSS (last row of the table). That is where the garbage goes; of the 12 GB left, 3.1 GB are the loader's and
  about 3.4 GB the machine's slabs that a 4 GB heap does not let the pool keep.
- **The targets are not reached.** Pauses are 9.7 % of the pass with the prototype (shared-2). At `-Xmx4g` the heap is 2.6
  GB live, 85 % of the collections are complete ones and their number follows the garbage only weakly (365 ->
  327 for 8 % less garbage).

## 6. The region question

*Is a worker's session better as ONE arena of ints that is reset by moving a pointer, with the tables sized once and
cleared by epoch? What prevents it, and what would a two-region design save?*

**It already is that arena, in two slabs.** Records (`vh`) and runs (`rs`) are filled by a bump pointer and a reset
moves the two pointers back; the stacks (`es`, `W`, `X`, `RS`, `SC`) are LIFO; nothing is cleared in the slabs (a
record and a run are written whole before they are read). What a reset does beyond two assignments is clear the
tables in O(used): one fill of the 128 KiB / 64 KiB base tables (or one probe per record when the session was
small), the pair entries through the log, an exchange of a grown table for the pooled base one. With the prototype
no reset and no growth allocates in steady state. Section 2.3 has the price: a reset is 8 - 9 us, 0.2 - 0.3 % of
the worker CPU. A single `int[]` for both slabs, or tables cleared by an epoch per slot (which needs a word per slot or bits
out of the 4-bit tag), cannot win more than that; **after (2) there is no gap for a pointer-reset arena to close,
so none was prototyped.** What costs more than the resets is table GROWTH: at the 1 MiB cap a session ends with
16,000 - 32,000 records (2,400 of 5,000 sessions on cedar), so nearly every session outgrows the 2^15-slot `vtab`
once and re-probes its records (2,666 of 5,058 sessions on cedar took the pooled 2^16 table; `rtab` 794, pairs
730): 4 - 6 % of the worker CPU is inside growths (section 2.3). "Sized once" in that sense is
`-Drlvm.machine.sizes=14,16,16,14,12,10` (a 256 KiB `vtab` from the start); that alone did not show in one row per
corpus (section 2.3), so the right base sizes are a sweep, not a conclusion.

**What prevents "everything a declaration made dies with it" is that warm sessions are worth having**, and the
survivors are not a small, known set. One worker, exact counters (`work/v1/tmp/batch-region.log`):

| | cedar: ticks | records | sessions | Std: ticks | records | sessions |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| one declaration per session (`gated,expect`: mark / release per declaration) | 163.5 M | 88.6 M | 145,789 | 127.7 M | 68.1 M | 100,907 |
| warm, cap 256 KiB | 153.9 M (-5.9 %) | 79.9 M | 11,474 | 120.8 M (-5.5 %) | 61.7 M | 8,339 |
| warm, cap 1 MiB (the default) | 137.7 M (-15.8 %) | 69.3 M (-21.8 %) | 4,956 | 106.4 M (-16.7 %) | 52.5 M (-22.9 %) | 3,223 |
| warm, cap 4 MiB | 121.0 M (-26.0 %) | 59.0 M (-33.3 %) | 2,839 | 91.3 M (-28.5 %) | 43.4 M (-36.3 %) | 1,561 |

At the default cap a declaration finds 22 % of the records it needs already made by an earlier declaration of its
session (133 of 613 per declaration on cedar) and saves 16 % of its ticks. Which records: X1's census
(`docs/research/profiles/X1.md`, table C2, `warm,gated,expect`): the tabling records of INFER / CHECK (39 - 58 %
found again), LAM closures (29 - 32 %), application spines (19 - 20 %), PI closures (16 - 19 %); the tabling
records of EVAL, the largest class, 7 - 18 %. They refer to each other by record id, so a survivor drags its
children along, and X1 found no cheap predictor at creation time.

**What a two-region design could save is therefore not memory but ticks**: the reuse of a 4 MiB session (-26 %
ticks against -16 %) at the table footprint of a 1 MiB one - F24 (NOTES-M6) measured that the 4 MiB session is
SLOWER in CPU time although it needs 12 % fewer ticks, because its tables leave the cache. A survivor region would
have to be filled by evacuation at the reset (copy the records that a LATER declaration found, with their children,
to the front; rebuild the tables; remap or drop every memo word that holds an id): a copying collector for the
machine's own heap, with a bit per record set on a cross-declaration hit. Upper bound of the gain: the 10 % of
ticks between the 1 MiB and the 4 MiB row, less the evacuation; in JVM garbage it saves nothing. Not prototyped;
it is an experiment for the value-store owners (M1 / X1's line of work), not a memory-management fix.

## 8. Not established

- **The two targets of the brief.** Collections under 2 % of a pipelined 8-worker pass on mathlib, and one process
  within 2 % of the 8-shard ceiling: not reached with the machine alone (section 5), and the ceiling itself was not
  measured again (P7: 26.6 - 26.8 s for a check-only pass; the quiet lock was 10 - 15 minutes per window, a ceiling
  run is eight mathlib loads at once). What keeps the pause share where it is after the workers' garbage is gone
  is the loader in `reload` passes (3.1 GB per load, and every young collection copies what it has just built)
  and, at `-Xmx4g`, a heap that is 2.6 GB live: neither is the machine's.
- **JVM launcher timings**: none. All timing rows are the native image; the JVM was used for the allocation
  account, the census, the gate and the invariance suite only.
- **One worker with the final prototype**: only the first prototype has one-worker rows (cedar, Std: inside the
  spread of three passes). A one-worker process has no shared collector to wait for; nothing is expected.
- **mathlib with the image's default heap** (13 GB): every mathlib row is `-Xmx4g`, where 85 % of the collections
  are complete ones; the pause times do not transfer to another heap setting.
- **The store and reference proposals** were measured in private copies (store: the published jar f85c730603b8 with
  the `Overlay` / `TermOps` classes of the copy, because LP2's working tree had unpublished changes in
  `ProofBlocks`). They changed no verdict in the runs here (cedar, Std, mathlib: ACCEPT; the unit tests of the
  two copies: section 7), but their owners' gates have not seen them.
- **Timing with the bounded store / reference pools**: the window (cedar, Std, mathlib) waited behind the
  measurement lock for more than ten minutes (the lock is not fair; waits of 15 - 30 minutes per window in the
  last hour) and was withdrawn when this report was closed. What is established for the bounded version: it
  passes mathlib at `-Xmx4g` with the base's peak RSS, with 249 collections and 12.1 GB freed where the base has
  365 and 20.7 GB (one pass on the loaded host); its wall clock is not measured. The timed `+ store / ref` rows
  of cedar and Std are the first, unbounded version.
- **mathlib, delivered tree against base in ONE window**: the delivered row (30.3 s) and the base row (31.4 s) are
  90 minutes apart; the same-window pair is shared-2 against base 40 minutes apart (29.9 / 31.4 s). The -4.8 % is
  therefore weaker evidence than the cedar and Std pairs; the CPU (217.6 -> 210 - 212 s) and the garbage (20.7 ->
  19.0 GB) agree in all three prototype rows.
- **Decay and bounds of the shared pool** (`big.decay` 8,192 resets, `big.mib`) are first guesses, not swept.
- **The first growth of the other modules' lanes** in every pass (store overlay, level overlays, reference kernel
  tables): seen in the account, not addressed.
- **A unit test for F-V1-M2** (the pair entry left by a refused growth of the log): none; the heap cannot be
  exhausted cheaply in a test.
