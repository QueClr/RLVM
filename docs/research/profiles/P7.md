# P7: where the wall clock of 8 workers goes

Agent "P7" (parallel scaling), 2026-11-24, 15:28Z to @@END@@. Question: the machine engine checked all of Mathlib
with 8 workers in 80 s of wall clock for 278 s of CPU on the loaded host, a speed-up of about 3.4, and 4 workers
were no slower. Where does the wall clock of a parallel run go, what of it is serial, and what can the driver
(`rlvm-driver`, which this agent owns since 15:33Z) do about it?

Short answer. On quiet cores the 3.4 was the host: the check phase scales 6.9x from 8 workers on Mathlib (7.4x
in a check-only pass), 6.9x on Std, 6.3x on cedar. What keeps a whole pipelined run at 5.5x - 5.9x is, in this
order: (1) **collections**: every pause of the single-threaded collector stops all 8 workers and the loader:
3.9 s of a 37.7 s pipelined run (10 %), 1.1 s of a 30.6 s check phase (3.5 %); the garbage is session arrays of
the machine and of the store's overlay that are grown again after each of 15,200 session resets, plus the
pipelined loader's; (2) **10 runnable threads on 8 cores** while the file loads (loader 12 s + environment thread
3 - 5 s of CPU are part of the job; on 8 cores they are 2 s of wall clock); (3) **the worker CPU itself grows**
by 6 % from 2 to 8 workers, which is the hardware's (eight one-worker processes over eight shards use the same
CPU: section 5); (4) on the mid-size corpora **one heavy unit** (a declined declaration
re-checked by the reference kernel) decides when the run ends. Nothing is lost to locks, to the frontier or to
the tail on Mathlib (parks 0.4 % - 1.1 %).

Method: section 1. Numbers: sections 2 - 5. What was changed in the driver and what it bought: section 6. What
was handed to other owners: section 7. The three next things: section 8.

## 1. Method

- **Quiet cores.** Every wall-clock number went through `bench/quiet.py` (the 8 performance cores 5-9,15-17
  reserved, one measurement at a time; between 16:02Z and 17:12Z through a private copy with a one-line parsing
  fix, same lock). The host's load average was 45 - 140 throughout; what leaks onto the measurement cores between
  two herding rounds shows as run-queue time of about 5 % in the 8-worker rows (the driver measures it, so it
  is known rather than hidden). Single runs unless a table says otherwise; differences below 3 % are not results.
- **Binary.** CE native image `-O3 --gc=serial`, `-X engine=machine -X machine.switches=warm,gated,expect`,
  session cap 4 MiB. Private distributions that differ only in the driver jar (`work/p7/tools/relink.sh`:
  the jars of `dist-a` + one driver jar, the image's flags as `scripts/build.sh` sets them):
  `dist-a` = the published jars of 15:31Z (driver c0aeb3288f46, machine 904d8ff41d25, store f85c730603b8, load
  26242def2701, admit 143aff8926eb); `dist-b` = + the time account (no behaviour change); `dist-c` = + a split
  bound, the kept pass, the parking rule; `dist-d` = + the shared-bytes fix; `dist-e` = + slices (what is
  published). JVM launcher rows: the launcher of `dist-d` with its default flags (13 GB heap, `-Xmn1g`, serial).
- **The driver's time account** (new; `rlvm-driver/README.md`, "Where the wall clock goes"): per worker, wall
  = on a CPU + runnable without a CPU (both from `/proc/thread-self/schedstat`) + parked in the scheduler by
  cause (nothing announced by the environment lane / the lane waits for the loader / tail: only busy claims
  left) + heavy gate + waiting for the end of the load + the rest (stopped by a collection, a page fault);
  the environment thread's account; time stamps; time series; slowest units; a per-unit trace.
  `rlvm-driver/bin/breakdown.py` prints it. Collections: `-XX:+PrintGC` of the image (`gcsum.py`), `-Xlog:gc,safepoint`
  on the JVM; allocation sites: JFR on the JVM launcher (every allocation outside a TLAB and every new TLAB).
- **Warm and check-only**: `RLVM_DRIVER=passes=N` (the driver's passes: 2..N check the loaded store again).
- **Hardware ceiling**: `-X shard=K/N` (new, measurement only): 8 one-worker processes over the 8 shards of
  the units, each pinned to one core, against one process with 8 workers.

Reading:

- **Collections are the serial part.** The pause time per check pass is the same at every worker count (about
  1 s; 1.8 s - 3.9 s with the pipelined loader's garbage on top), so its share of the wall clock is 0.5 % with
  one worker and 3.5 % (batch) / 10.3 % (pipelined) with 8. A check pass frees 14 GB (20 KB per unit, 0.9 MB per
  session reset) whatever the worker count; the pipelined run frees 35.6 GB.
- **Run queue.** Batch, 8 workers: 7.5 % = the environment thread (4.85 s of CPU during the first 6.3 s) +
  about 5 % of host noise. Pipelined: loader + environment thread + 8 workers on 8 cores for 21.65 s (the load
  alone takes 12.06 s): 13.6 %.
- **The workers never wait for the loader or the frontier**: loaded 334,030 units at 10 s, announced 333,268,
  accepted 204,673; parks 0.1 %. Environment thread: 2.3 s inside the pass + 4.0 s adding up
  `Store.sharedBytes()` after each of its 12,744 steps (`DeclTable.claimBytes` walks one slot per unit: 0.3 ms):
  a driver bug, fixed (section 6).
- **No unit and no chain bounds Mathlib**: slowest unit 2.15 s (376698, a declined unit re-checked by the
  reference kernel), then 0.74 s; the heaviest unit holds 0.2 % of the machine's steps; the work is spread
  evenly along the file (10 % of the steps in the first 12.8 % of the units, 90 % in the first 86.8 %); "frontier
  complete" to "last unit checked" is simply the rest of the run, and the tail proper is 0.1 - 0.25 s per worker.
- **Worker CPU**: 201 s with 2 workers, 205 s with 4, 213 s with 8 (batch); pipelined 206 / 211 / 224 s.
  Eight one-worker processes over eight shards use the same 210 - 213 s (section 5): the growth is the
  hardware's (eight busy cores), not the process's.

- **Std with 8 workers is bounded by one unit.** Unit 23249 is the largest proof of the file (76,204 block
  nodes), is declined by the machine and re-checked by the reference kernel: 0.7 s on a quiet core, 1.09 s when
  ten threads share eight cores. 8 workers have 1.5 s of CPU each, and the unit sits at about half of the work.
  Share of the tail: 5.8 % (batch), 17.6 % (pipelined), and 27.7 % in a check-only pass of the old scheduler,
  which cut the whole file into 8 contiguous eighths: the unit sat near the end of its eighth and started at
  1.4 s of a run that could have ended at 1.5 s.

  is the length of the run's first part; the gain is the load's CPU time moved onto idle cores (2 - 4 workers:
  all of it; 8 workers: a third).

## 5. The hardware ceiling

`-X shard=K/8` makes a one-worker process check only shard K of the units (blocks of 1,024 units, block number
modulo 8) after a complete load and environment pass; passes 2 and 3 are check-only. Eight such processes, each
pinned to one of the eight measurement cores (`work/p7/tools/ceiling.sh mathlib`, dist-e, 17:35Z), against one
process with eight workers:

| | check-only pass, wall | CPU |
|---|--:|--:|
| 8 one-worker processes at once, per shard | 23.7 / 24.2 / 25.1 / 25.2 / 26.7 / 28.7 / 29.3 / 31.2 s (mean 26.8 s; third pass mean 26.6 s) | sum 213.0 s (third pass 210.7 s) |
| the same shard alone on its core (shards 0 and 4) | 23.89 s and 22.21 s (at once: 25.14 s and 23.71 s) | at once +5.2 % and +6.7 % |
| one process, 8 workers | 27.9 - 28.7 s | workers 209.5 - 212.7 s |

- **One process costs no more CPU than eight**: 210 - 213 s either way. Nothing that the workers share (store
  pages, the fact store, the scheduler, the verdict board) shows up as CPU.
- **Eight busy cores make each of them 5 % - 7 % slower** (memory bandwidth and the shared cache; the store is
  2.1 GB of mostly random reads): this is the growth of the worker CPU from 2 to 8 workers (201 -> 213 s) and
  it is the hardware's. So the ceiling of this machine for a check-only pass over Mathlib is about 26.6 s
  (the mean shard time at once = a perfectly balanced 8-way split), i.e. 7.6 - 7.9 times one worker.
- **One process with 8 workers is 5 % - 8 % above that ceiling** (27.9 - 28.7 s): the collections (1.0 - 1.5 s
  of stop-the-world, which eight separate heaps do not share) and the host's noise. A static split is worse
  than the scheduler: the shards differ by 30 % (23.7 s to 31.2 s), so eight independent processes end after
  31.2 s.

All in `rlvm-driver` (jar @@JAR@@, published @@PUB@@); tests 106 (103 run green, 3 opt-in). Default behaviour and every
verdict are unchanged; what changed is who checks a unit and when, what the statistics say, and what a
pipelined run decides for files that are not in declaration order (now what the batch run decides).

## 8. The next three things for the 8-worker wall clock

In the order of what they would bring on Mathlib with 8 workers (the driver's own waits being 1 %):

1. **Do not make the garbage** (P7-F1; machine and store owners): a session reset that keeps its arrays, a
   worker context that does not copy the level tables at every snapshot, a re-check lane that keeps its
   overlay. The collector is the one serial component whose share grows with the worker count: 1.1 s of a
   28 - 30 s check phase, 3.9 s of a 35 - 38 s pipelined run today, and the 5 % - 8 % that separate one process
   from the hardware ceiling. A larger young generation does not do it (26 collections of 123 ms instead of
   403 of 10 ms: each one copies the live session arrays).
2. **The units the machine declines** (P7-F3; machine owners): 0.94 % of Mathlib's units take 10.7 % of the
   time inside units (measured on the loaded host, so indicative) at reference-kernel speed: about 20 s of the
   213 s of worker CPU, up to 2.5 s of wall clock with 8 workers; on Std one such unit is the end of the run. In the driver, the companion
   is to start large units first (the size predicts the cost; `rlvm-driver/README.md`, "Open", has the
   progress rule such a scheduler must keep): Std with 8 workers pipelined about 2.0 -> 1.7 s, Mathlib nothing.
3. **The warm JVM** (the platform question of owner note 12, for the steward): a check-only pass over Mathlib
   with 8 workers takes 23.6 s on the JVM launcher against 28.4 s in the native image (worker CPU 174 s
   against 210 s: C2's code), for 6 - 11 GB of resident set against 3.6 - 4.0 GB; cold, the two are equal
   (42.6 s batch, 34.6 s pipelined). Where steady state counts, this is 17 % at once, more than anything the
   scheduler can still give.

Not on the list because it is not serial but simply work: loader (12 s) and environment thread (1.6 - 3 s) are
15 s of CPU, 1.9 s of wall clock on 8 cores, and with 8 workers on 8 cores they are why a pipelined run has ten
runnable threads for 20 s. On a machine with ten cores the pipelined run would be the check-only time.
