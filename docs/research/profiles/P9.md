# P9 - The gap to the leaders on navier-stokes-euler, cslib and con-leche, declaration by declaration

Profiler P9, 2026-11-24 (21:08Z - 21:55Z). No production code was changed. Scratch, tools and raw data:
`work/prof/P9/`. Living summary: `work/status/prof-P9.md`. Board lines: `work/status/requests.log` (21:16Z, 21:26Z,
21:5xZ). Model and tools: profile P8 (`docs/research/profiles/P8.md`, `work/prof/P8/w9/`).

Question: the machine's CPU time against gruszka (integrator's table, fresh process) is 3.9 times on
navier-stokes-euler, 1.8 times on cslib, 1.3 times on con-leche. P8 explained Mathlib's 3.0 times by the official
way of comparing proofs (40 % of the machine's work) and by the eager-reduction declines (10-12 %). What is it on
navier-stokes-euler: the same two mechanisms in another proportion, or something new? And does a class of
declarations stick out on cslib and con-leche?

(One worker, native -O3, the machine's default switch set of the published jars of 21:02Z, instructions and thread
CPU inside `Session.check` per unit; gruszka and nano-lean per declaration with the arena builds, measured in the
same hour; CPU shares from one `bench/quiet.py` window, section 4.8.)

1. **navier-stokes-euler is the same mechanism as Mathlib in a larger dose; nothing about numerals.** 54.5 % of the
   machine's instructions there (51 % of the worker's CPU time on quiet cores: 29.7 of 58.1 s) disappear with the
   two non-official options; with them the machine is at 3.63 times gruszka, which is where Mathlib ends up (3.88)
   and where con-leche and cslib are without them (3.2).
2. **Where**: 84 % of the machine's instructions are in the PROJECT'S OWN 57,330 units, at 9.35 times gruszka (its
   Mathlib part: 4.63, Init: 2.58). Worst: the 690 `Euler*` namespaces (25,646 units, 57.7 % of the machine's
   instructions, 13.4 times). Units at 8 times and more are 14.2 % of the units and 61.6 % of the machine's
   instructions (19.8 times as a class); the units below 8 are at 4.08 times - the same figure as on Mathlib.
3. **What is compared**: (i) proofs by their types (the official rule), as on Mathlib, but the proofs are those of
   analysis: ℝ's two instance paths (`Real.normedField ..` against `Real.semiring` / `Real.commRing`), bundled maps
   (`LinearMap.instAdd._proof_1/2`, `ContinuousLinearMap.add._proof_1`, `MultilinearMap`, `BoundedContinuousFunction`)
   and subobjects (`AddMemClass.add._proof_1`: elements of `Lp`); (ii) a second class that `skipargs` does not
   touch and only `congruence` repairs, 14 % of the corpus: applications of ONE HEAD or ONE TERM UNDER TWO
   ENVIRONMENTS, unfolded on both sides instead of compared by their arguments. The extreme unit
   (`EulerSobolevBaseCommutator.baseCommutator_bound`, 1.54 M ticks against gruszka's 13 k) compares a sum over a
   list of 5,461 Sobolev words with itself under another environment, element by element (section 4.2).
4. **What it is not**: Nat / Int literal arithmetic is 70,600 folding events and recursor reduction 277,300 IOTA
   events of 553 M (nano-lean counts 277,600 iota steps and 57,500 literal operations, 12 of them on big numbers);
   units in which recursors and literals are 3 % or more of the events carry 0.3 % of the machine's
   instructions; structure eta is 12,400 ETAS events. The large `Finset.sum` / `Matrix.vecCons` proofs
   (`quadraticBilin._proof_N`, the top 4 units for everybody) are at 3.7 times with the same number of
   operations as gruszka (section 4.6): cost per operation, not a missing rule. A sum is the cost in one unit
   only, and there it is the conversion of point 3 (ii).
5. **Eager-reduction declines are small on navier-stokes-euler (251 units, 0.87 s of 58 s) and as large as on
   Mathlib on cslib: 2,406 units, 5.9 s of 46.6 s (12.6 % of the worker)**, for units that cost gruszka 1.0 s.
6. **cslib is Mathlib at a smaller dose, nothing sticks out**: 79 % of the cost is in the 297,351 units of Mathlib
   and its dependencies (4.39 times; the options remove 27.5 % there), the project's own 14,249 units are 3 % of
   the cost at 2.63 times. **con-leche is flat**: every kind of unit with more than 1 % of the cost between 2.6 and 3.9 times, the machine makes
   1.30 times gruszka's ticks and 0.62 times its values; the factor is instructions per operation (1,979 per tick
   against 793). One unit is 5.2 % of the corpus (`ConLeche.Cached.reduceNatI._mutual.eq_def`, 7.9 % for gruszka).
7. **The two options are not a free win**: on con-leche `congruence` costs 8.4 % of the instructions (that one unit
   goes from 7.65 G to 20.67 G: the congruence walks cost more than the unfoldings they replace, section 4.7).
   Whoever weighs an opt-in profile needs that row.
8. **In CPU time the ratios are smaller than in instructions** because the machine retires 15 to 18 G instructions
   per second inside the checks and gruszka 6 to 7: about 3.2 (navier-stokes-euler), 1.6 (cslib), 1.2 (con-leche)
   for the checks alone (gruszka's side is its own clock from the profile run, not a quiet measurement).

Numbers are instruction and event counts unless a unit of time is given. CPU seconds are thread CPU clocks of one
`bench/quiet.py` window (section 4.8) unless marked otherwise.

## 6. What could not be established

- **gruszka's CPU time per unit on quiet cores.** Its instructions and counters per declaration are exact; the
  seconds quoted for it (0.13 s, 1.04 s, 0.72 s, the 6 - 7 G instructions per second) are its own clock from the
  profile run in a container on CPUs 18 and 19 while the host was loaded, with a marker line per declaration.
  The CPU ratios of point 8 of section 0 are therefore directions; the integrator's table is the quiet comparison
  of the whole processes.
- **The machine with E1's fixes** (jar 5945b6568177 of 21:26Z and what follows): everything here is jar
  15cb3777e598. The fixes change inference; I did not measure them.
- **The warm wall clock with 8 workers**: everything is one worker, cold process, batch load.
- **How much of class 4.2 is B12 (closure congruence) and how much B1 (same-head probe)**: the two are one option.
  The trace shows the closure case; the counters of the options run say B1 answered T 4.1 M times and B12 0.9 M
  times, which is not a cost split.
- **Whether an official-exact form of 4.1 / 4.2 exists**: C1's report says no for 4.1; 4.2 ("entries convertible,
  not identical") is the same question for closures and I did not examine it.
- **What the reference fallback's 2.4 ms per declined unit consists of** (it is not proportional to the reference
  steps): engine start, import of the unit, the steps themselves were not separated.
- **Instructions of the inductive blocks on the machine's side** (only thread CPU of the gaps), and which blocks.
- **The session cap in CPU time on navier-stokes-euler and cslib**; con-leche only (section 4.5).
- The origin classification uses the arena's `mathlib` export as "Mathlib and its dependencies"; 683 units of
  navier-stokes-euler are private names of Mathlib modules that are not in that export (another Mathlib version) and
  were assigned by their module name.
