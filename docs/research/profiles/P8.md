# P8 - The gap to the leaders on Mathlib, declaration by declaration

Profiler P8, 2026-11-24 (15:30Z - 17:00Z). No production code was changed. Scratch, tools and raw data:
`work/prof/P8/w9/`. Living summary: `work/status/prof-P8.md`. Board lines: `work/status/requests.log` (16:20Z,
16:35Z, later ones).

Question: the machine checks Mathlib at about 4 times gruszka's CPU time and Init at about 2.7 times. Why is
Mathlib worse, and what do the leaders do on the declarations where we lose most?

## 0. Answer

1. **In instructions the machine is 6.5 times gruszka on Mathlib and 4.7 times on Init** (per unit, inside
   `Session.check`, joined by name over 714,712 units: 2,438.6 G against 373.8 G; nano-lean 319.3 G: 7.6 times).
   The CPU-time ratio looks smaller only because the machine retires about twice as many instructions per second
   as gruszka (13.6 G/s against 6.1 G/s; both on CPUs 18 and 19 in the arena container, gruszka's per-declaration
   profile by the baseline team the night before).
2. **The whole Mathlib-specific part is one class of work: conversion of proofs.** 48 % of the machine's
   instructions on Mathlib are in 142,000 units (20 % of the units) in which the machine needs 8 to 200 times
   gruszka's instructions (18 times as a class). The other 80 % of the units are at 4.1 times, the Init ratio.
   In those 142,000 units the machine runs 10 to 93 definition unfoldings per unfolding of gruszka; over the
   whole corpus inference is at parity (INFER + CHECK events are 0.98 of gruszka's infer misses).
3. **Mechanism**: two instance paths that meet (`Semiring.toAddCommMonoid ..` against
   `AddCommGroup.toAddCommMonoid ..`) are compared field by field; every proof field, and every proof argument of
   two applications of one constant, goes through proof irrelevance, which, as in the official kernel, compares
   the TYPES of the two proofs: the statements of `add_comm`, `mul_zero`, `left_distrib`, ..., in which every
   `+`, `*` and `0` is unfolded again on both sides. gruszka and nano-lean never compare a proof argument or a
   proof field, and they try the arguments of two applications of one head before unfolding it.
4. **The machine already contains both shortcuts and keeps them off on purpose** (`-Drlvm.machine.m4=
   congruence,skipargs`, NOTES-M4 D27): the soundness review showed hostile exports on which they give a verdict the
   official checker does not give (S-F1 / S-F2), and owner note 11 says "resemble official by default". Measured
   on all of Mathlib (every unit accepted in every run):

   | switches (native -O3, 1 worker, machine jar a5313e0b6c32) | ticks | instructions in checks | against gruszka |
   |---|--:|--:|--:|
   | `warm,gated,expect` (the published step-1 set) | 1,511.5 M | 2,438.6 G | 6.52 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,facts` (official-exact) | 1,436.1 M | 2,410.5 G | 6.45 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,facts,probes` (official-exact) | 1,449.8 M | 2,435.1 G | 6.51 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,probes,code,casetrees,lazyeval,k2` (the rich gated set) | 1,367.7 M | 2,212.5 G | 5.92 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,facts` + `skipargs` (NOT official-exact) | 876.8 M | 1,673.7 G (-31.4 %) | 4.48 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,facts,probes` + `congruence` (NOT official-exact) | 807.6 M | 1,640.5 G (-32.7 %) | 4.39 |
   | `warm,gated,expect,facts,probes` + `congruence,skipargs` (NOT official-exact) | 680.1 M | **1,450.5 G (-40.5 %)** | 3.88 |

   The two options overlap: each alone removes about a third, because they answer the same comparisons from two
   sides (`skipargs` does not pose them; `congruence` answers them by the same-head probe and from the slots of the
   two frames instead of unfolding).

   With them the machine is at 3.9 times gruszka on Mathlib, every class of unit and every library area between 3.3
   and 4.3, and the distribution of the per-unit ratio is as narrow as on Init. On Init, Std and cedar the same
   options are worth 3.6 %, 5.0 % and 6.0 %: the mechanism is specific to Mathlib's algebraic hierarchy and
   category theory.
5. **So the answer to "why is Mathlib worse" is a policy, not a defect**: 40 % of the machine's Mathlib time is
   the price of comparing proofs exactly as the official kernel does. Nothing that is official-exact and switched
   off today recovers it (`facts`, `probes`: 0.1 % to 1.2 % of the instructions; the rich gated set 9 %).
   Either the owner accepts verdicts that are sound by the theory but not the official algorithm's on hostile
   exports (as both leaders do), or an official-exact way to answer these comparisons cheaply has to be found
   (section 4.1 says what the trace suggests and what I could not establish).
6. **Second finding: the eager-reduction decline rule (M6-F26) costs 11 to 12 % of the worker's CPU time on
   Mathlib.** 6,711 units (0.94 %) are declined and checked by the reference engine: 23.5 s of thread CPU against
   177.7 s for the 708,001 units the machine accepts. For gruszka they are 6.3 % of the corpus (3.5 s).
7. **What remains after both is uniform**: 1.5 times gruszka's ticks, 1.3 times its values, 2.6 times its
   instructions per tick, in every class. That part is not about what is computed but about what one operation
   costs (profilers P1, P5, P6).

Numbers of this page are instruction and event counts unless a unit of time is given; the CPU seconds are thread
CPU clocks of one container on CPUs 18 and 19 of a loaded host and are NOT quiet-host measurements (section 7).

## 4. Mechanisms

### 4.1 Proofs are compared by their types (the official rule); the leaders do not compare them at all

**Size: 31.4 % of the machine's Mathlib instructions (42 % of its ticks) for the proof positions alone; 40.5 % (55 %)
together with 4.2; the two overlap (4.2 alone: 32.7 %).** Owner of the place: M4 (`Conv.java`, `Cargs.java`), facts by M5 (`Facts.propMask`); the rule
that keeps it off: NOTES-M4 D27, findings S-F1 / S-F2 of the soundness review, owner note 11.

Trace (`out/trace-scheme-s1.txt`; unit `AlgebraicGeometry.Spec.sheafedSpaceMap_hom_c_app`):

| | ticks / steps | unfoldings | conversion problems | inference |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| machine `warm,gated,expect` | 65,363 | 5,359 DELTA | 2,227 CONV + 1,868 pair hits, 2,086 PIRR, 3,383 VTYPE | 132 INFER, 5 CHECK |
| machine + `facts` | 62,106 | 5,359 | 892 PIRR, 2,001 VTYPE | |
| machine + `facts` + `skipargs` | 8,091 | 589 | 298 CONV, 29 PIRR | 130 INFER |
| machine + `facts,probes` + `congruence,skipargs` | 3,849 | 221 | 187 CONV, 10 PIRR | |
| gruszka | 222 | 10 | 25 def_eq, 14 cache hits | 33 infer misses |
| nano-lean | 162 | 9 | 82 def_eq, 76 pointer-equal | 21 infer misses |

What happens (events 15 to 50 of the trace): while the type of the theorem is inferred, `Module R R inst ..` is
compared with `Module R R inst' ..`; the instance arguments are `Semiring.toAddCommMonoid R (CommSemiring.toSemiring
..)` and `AddCommGroup.toAddCommMonoid R (Ring.toAddCommGroup ..)`. Lazy delta unfolds both, structure eta (ETAS)
compares the fields; field 1 is `add_comm`, a proof: CONV, not identical, PIRR, and PIRR, as
`is_def_eq_proof_irrel`, infers both types and compares them: `(a b : R) -> a + b = b + a` with `+` through one
path against the same with `+` through the other. `HAdd.hAdd`, `instHAdd`, `Add.add`, `AddMonoid.toAdd` are
projection functions (reducibility hint abbrev), so the official order does not try their arguments first: both
sides are unfolded down to the structure. That repeats for every occurrence of an operation in every law of every
structure on the path. The 5,359 DELTA events of this unit: 610 `Mul.mul`, 590 `HMul.hMul`, 360 `HAdd.hAdd`, 360
`Add.add`, 172 `OfNat.ofNat`, 147 `Membership.mem`, 104 `AddCommMonoid.toAddMonoid`, 104 `instHMul` ...; the host
stacks: `DELTA<LSTEP<LAZYD<CONV<CARGS<CONV<CBIND<CONV<PIRR<CONV<CARGS` (910), `..<CONV<ETAS<..`. The conversion
problems by head: 230 `HMul.hMul =?= HMul.hMul`, 209 Pi, 194 lambda, 181 `Eq`, 148 `HAdd.hAdd`, 102 `Exists`, and the
proofs themselves: `Semiring.mul_zero =?= Semiring.mul_zero` (18), `Semiring.left_distrib` (16) ...

The extreme cases are instance fields: `AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.instAddCommGroupEllAdicCohomology._proof_21` costs
the machine 2,204,336 ticks and 2.91 G instructions, gruszka 144 ticks and 90,700 instructions (32,000 times); with
`skipargs` 604 ticks, with `congruence,skipargs` 175. Twenty-odd `_proof_N` / `_aux_N` units of that one instance
are 45 G instructions, 1.8 % of the machine's Mathlib.

What the leaders do instead:

- gruszka, `refs/gruszka/src/tc.rs`: `spines_defeq_skip` (8573-8600) compares the arguments of two applications of
  one constant except the positions of `prop_positions(row, levels)` (8641: positions whose declared domain is
  surely a proposition) and, inside lazy delta, of `unused_args(row)` (8603: positions the body never reads);
  `try_eta_struct` (8823) skips the proof fields of the constructor the same way; `surely_not_proof` (7297) answers
  "is it a proof" without inference; `proof_irrel` (7499) is reached only for what is left.
- nano-lean, `refs/nano-lean/src/value_checker/conv.rs`: `relevant` (389) is the same mask (unused and Prop-typed
  arguments), `probe_args` (359) tries the arguments of two applications of the same head BEFORE `whnf_core` and
  before proof irrelevance, for every unfoldable head (the official kernel only for `regular` hints, inside lazy
  delta), with a step budget; `statically_not_proof` (315).
- The machine has the same three things: the comparison plan of CARGS as a skip mask (B3 proof positions, B4 unread
  positions), the Prop mask of ETAS (`Conv.etasFields`), static PROOF (B7). B7 is in `facts` and is official-exact;
  B3, B4 and the ETAS mask need `-Drlvm.machine.m4=skipargs` since D27.

Is it official-exact? **No, and that is documented**: the soundness review (S-F2) and M4 hold exports that the
official checker rejects and that these shortcuts accept (the official `is_def_eq` is not a congruence where a
reduction inspects a proof; `M4OfficialTest` pins three of them). The equations the shortcuts assume are valid in
the theory, so this is "sound by the theory, not the official algorithm's verdict"; both leaders are in that
position. It is not a soundness hole of the kind that proves `False`, as far as the review and M4's notes say; I did
not re-examine that.

What an official-exact form would have to do, from the trace: the expensive comparisons are of the form T[A] against
T[B], where T is the same domain term (the statement of a law) and A, B are two instance paths whose comparison is
already in the pair table as POS. The official kernel has no better tool for this either (its success cache holds
exact pairs only since v4.35, research 02 section 5; it needs 30 times gruszka's instructions on Mathlib, and how
much of that is this class I did not measure). Candidates the owners could examine, none of which I verified: (a) when the two types are
the same term under two frames, compare by walking the term once with the frames in lockstep and consult the pair
table at the slots (`Same` of M4, D28, does the walk but answers only for identical slots); the question is whether
"slots POS, term identical" implies the official verdict for PROPOSITIONS that contain no proof-inspecting
reduction, which is the case M4 could not close in general; (b) the comparison of the two unfolded sides repeats
the same unfold-to-structure steps for each occurrence: a memo of "DELTA + CORE of `HAdd.hAdd A x y` is `proj Add 0
(toAdd A) x y`" per (head, instance arguments) would keep the official steps and remove most of the events - the
steps are official, only their cost changes. (b) is engineering inside the official rule; its size is bounded by
the 31 % and I could not measure it without building it. That `congruence` ALONE (the proofs' types are still
compared, but the same-head probe B1 and the closure congruence B12 answer before anything is unfolded) removes
32.7 % says that the cost is in how T[A] against T[B] is decided, not
in the fact that it is asked: candidate (a) is exactly the part of `congruence` that would have to be proven exact
for propositions.

### 4.2 Congruence before unfolding

**Size: 32.7 % of the machine's Mathlib instructions alone (2,435.1 G -> 1,640.5 G with `facts,probes`; ticks
1,449.8 M -> 807.6 M), 9 % on top of 4.1** (1,673.7 G -> 1,450.5 G). Owner: M4 (`Conv.congruence`: B1, B2, B12). Off
since D27 for the same reason (S-F1).

gruszka: `closure_congr` (`tc.rs` 6792) and `same_term` (7026): two closures of the same term and level
substitution whose READ environment entries are pairwise convertible are equal; tried after every delta step.
nano-lean: `same(t, s, fuel 512)` (`conv.rs` 107) and the same-head probe. In
`AlgebraicGeometry.Scheme.SpecMap_presheaf_map_eqToHom` the machine's DELTA events go 16,140 -> 1,564 (`skipargs`) ->
137 (`congruence,skipargs`), and 137 is exactly gruszka's unfold count for that unit; ticks 186,343 -> 21,932 -> 5,243
against gruszka's 4,208. With both options the machine's event counts are within a factor 1.5 of gruszka's in the
units I looked at.

### 4.3 Eager-reduction declines (M6-F26): the reference engine checks Mathlib's heaviest proofs

**Size: 23.5 s of 210 s of the worker's thread CPU (11 to 12 %), up to 20 s of it recoverable.** Owner: M6
(`MachineSession`, `Machine.enterER`), armed by M5 / M7.

6,711 units enter eager reduction (an argument `eagerReduce _ _`); 6,538 are `_proof_N` theorems, 6,017 under
`Mathlib.*`, 400 under `Batteries.*`. The machine spends 1.2 s (9.5 M ticks, 15.3 G instructions) before declining
them, declines each twice (the driver's cold retry is answered at once), and the reference engine decides them:
66.5 M reference steps, 23.5 s of thread CPU between the checks of those units. For gruszka the same units are
23.9 G instructions = 6.3 % of its corpus and 3.5 s, for nano-lean 7.6 %; they contain the most expensive
declarations of Mathlib for both leaders (`..sym2x_add_sub_eq_addSubMap_sym2x._proof_1_8`: 1.89 G instructions for
gruszka; `IsEllipticNet.atomRel_of_even_odd._proof_1_15`; `SzemerediRegularity.edgeDensity_star_not_uniform._proof_1_10`).
On Init 164 units, on Std 203, on cedar 423 are declined the same way. Official-exactness is the reason for the
rule (the official answer under `eagerReduce` depends on per-declaration caches); the cost was not in any table.
With several workers these units are also the longest ones of the run, so the fallback sets the tail of the wall
clock (P7's topic; not measured here).

The other 708,001 units cost 10.5 microseconds of thread CPU each between two checks (7.5 s, 3.5 % of the worker):
the driver's bookkeeping, admission and 40,568 session resets.

### 4.5 Sessions

On the Mathlib slice the session cap moves the ticks by at most 16 %: 1 MiB 31.7 M, 4 MiB (default) 26.9 M, 16 MiB
24.6 M, 64 MiB 23.0 M, 1 GiB 22.7 M. A larger warm session is therefore not the Mathlib factor (and costs CPU time
through cache misses, as the integrator measured on Init). Units that gruszka answers almost for free because an
earlier declaration of the session inferred the same closed term (e.g. `List.MergeSort.Internal.splitRevInTwo.
_proof_2`: 5 ticks for gruszka, 48,358 for the machine) exist in every corpus; after `congruence,skipargs` the
units at 64 times and more are 1.3 % of the machine's Mathlib instructions. That is the upper bound of what shared
check facts (`pchk`, and its extension to terms with level parameters, which most of Mathlib's are) can still
bring; I did not separate it further.

### 4.6 What remains: the cost of one operation

With `congruence,skipargs` (Mathlib): 680.1 M ticks against gruszka's 454.4 M (1.50), 345.2 M records against
263.9 M values interned (1.31), 2,133 instructions per tick against 823 (2.6), 4,200 instructions per record
against 1,416 values. The ratio per class of unit is between 3.3 and 5.5, per library area between 2.2 and 6.2
(the large areas; the largest: the `Init` part of the export at 6.2, `_proof_N` units of Init that evaluate). This
is the same gap that Init shows (4.7 in instructions for the whole process; 90.9 G inside the checks against about
20.5 G for gruszka's checking, i.e. about 4.4), and it is the subject of the
function-level profiles (hash-consing probes, PRUNE, frames), not of this report.

## 6. What could not be established

- **CPU seconds on a quiet host for Mathlib.** The CPU-time figures of this report (178.9 s for the checks of the plain
  set, 108.8 s with the two options, 23.5 s of fallback) are thread CPU clocks from single runs in a container on CPUs
  18 and 19 while the host was loaded (load average 50 to 140). Counts and instructions are exact; the seconds are
  directions. A short quiet window on the Mathlib slice is in section 7.
- **Whether an official-exact form of mechanisms 1 and 2 exists**, and how much of the 40 % it would keep. Section 4.1
  names two candidates; neither was built or checked against the hostile exports.
- **gruszka's instructions per unit on cedar and Std** (its instruction profile exists for Mathlib only); the contrast
  rows use its tick counter.
- **How the 23.5 s of the eager-reduction fallback split** between the reference engine and the resets around it,
  and its effect on the wall clock with workers.
- **The split of the remaining instructions by judgment** (events are exact, instructions per event were not
  regressed).
- The private trace build was made from the working tree of 16:05Z, which is later than the measured jar; on the
  slice it counts 31.6 M ticks where the measured jar counts 26.9 M (the traced units agree to 0.1 %). I did not find
  out which later change costs 17 % of the ticks on that slice; somebody who published between 15:34Z and 16:05Z
